**Q1.** [Domain I.A | Bloom: Understand] ⚠️ CLOSE-PAIR

A machine learning engineer explains two distinct properties of a deep learning credit scoring model to the bank's compliance team. She describes *Characteristic X* as an inherent structural property of the model architecture: even the development team cannot fully trace which combination of input features produced a specific output score. She describes *Characteristic Y* as a consequential governance challenge: the bank is currently unable to provide an applicant with a human-understandable account of why their credit application was declined, even when the applicant formally requests a reason.

Which of the following CORRECTLY identifies Characteristics X and Y?

A) Characteristic X is complexity, because deep learning models contain too many parameters for humans to reason about; Characteristic Y is autonomy, because the model operated independently of human input when generating the declined decision

B) Characteristic X is opacity (lack of interpretability), a structural property of how certain model architectures process information internally; Characteristic Y is low explainability, the consequential inability to communicate meaningful reasoning about model outputs to affected individuals

C) Characteristic X is explainability, because the model's internal mechanics cannot be explained even to its developers; Characteristic Y is opacity, because the model's output logic cannot be made transparent to external stakeholders on request

D) Characteristic X is data dependency, because the model's entire decision logic is encoded in its training data; Characteristic Y is probabilistic output uncertainty, because any credit score represents a statistical likelihood rather than a certain outcome

**Q2.** [Domain I.A | Bloom: Analyze] — Case Study

A consumer credit bureau deploys an AI model that processes 400,000 loan applications per month across 12 partner banks. Eight months after deployment, a routine audit reveals that the model has been systematically assigning lower credit scores to applicants from postal code clusters with above-average minority population concentrations — a bias present since day one of deployment.

Which statement BEST illustrates how a unique characteristic of AI DIRECTLY amplified the societal harm in this scenario compared to an equivalent bias in a manual underwriting process?

A) The model's capacity to process decisions at scale and speed meant the bias affected approximately 3.2 million credit decisions across 12 institutions before detection — a systemic harm magnitude that would have been impossible in manual underwriting operating at human pace across individual desks

B) The model's data dependency meant that the bias was baked into the model weights at training time and could only be corrected through a full retraining cycle, making it structurally slower to remediate than a procedural error in a manual process

C) The model's opacity meant that affected applicants had no legal standing to challenge their credit decisions, since they could not prove the scoring was discriminatory without access to the model's internal architecture

D) The model's probabilistic output design meant that the bureau could plausibly attribute any individual adverse decision to statistical chance rather than discriminatory criteria, providing a shield against individual discrimination claims

**Q3.** [Domain I.A | Bloom: Analyze]

The hospital has deployed it as a "decision support tool" — governance documentation explicitly requires mandatory independent radiologist review. However, in practice, when the system outputs a confidence score above 90%, radiologists consistently proceed directly to treatment recommendations without documenting any independent clinical assessment. Following a patient complaint after an unnecessary surgical intervention, the hospital's legal team cannot determine from records whether the treating radiologist exercised clinical judgment or simply acted on the AI output.

Which governance control would MOST directly have prevented this accountability gap?

A) Replacing the numerical confidence score with a binary "flag / no flag" output, eliminating the psychological anchoring effect of high-percentage scores that leads radiologists to defer to the model

B) Requiring the imaging system vendor to provide certified calibration data demonstrating that confidence scores accurately reflect actual diagnostic accuracy rates across all patient demographics

C) Implementing a post-deployment monitoring dashboard that tracks the correlation between AI confidence scores and final patient outcomes, enabling identification of systematic over-reliance patterns

D) Requiring radiologists to complete and submit a mandatory structured assessment field documenting their independent clinical reasoning before the system reveals its confidence score — creating an auditable record that separates the clinician's judgment from the AI recommendation

**Q5.** [Domain II.A | Bloom: Apply] ⚠️ CLOSE-PAIR

An insurance company uses a fully automated AI system to process motor vehicle claims. When a claim is denied, the affected policyholder receives only a standardized letter stating: *"Your claim has been assessed and does not meet the criteria for approval."* No explanation of the logic used is provided, no human reviewer is involved, and the policyholder is given no avenue to challenge the outcome.

Which data protection right is MOST directly violated by this process?

A) The right not to be subject to solely automated decision-making with legal or similarly significant effects — which entitles the individual to request human intervention, receive a meaningful explanation of the logic and significance of the decision, and express their point of view before the decision is finalized

B) The right of access, because the policyholder is entitled to receive a complete copy of all personal data the organization holds about them — including every input variable, weighting factor, and intermediate score used by the AI model in processing their specific claim

C) The right to data portability, because the policyholder should be able to obtain the personal data used in their claim assessment in a structured, machine-readable format and transmit it to another insurer for an independent assessment

D) The right to restriction of processing, because the automated denial constitutes an active processing decision that the policyholder has the right to challenge and suspend pending a formal human review of accuracy

**Q6.** [Domain III.A | Bloom: Evaluate] — Case Study

A hospital system is preparing to deploy an AI-powered patient triage system in its emergency department. During the pre-deployment impact assessment, the team identifies a material risk: the model may systematically under-prioritize patients from demographic groups underrepresented in the training dataset. The governance team is evaluating four proposed risk mitigations:

- **Option 1:** Add a disclaimer visible to triage nurses stating that AI triage outputs are advisory and may not accurately reflect all patient presentations
- **Option 2:** Require a senior clinician to review and sign off on all AI-generated triage priority assignments before they are acted upon
- **Option 3:** Augment the training dataset with representative patient data from underrepresented populations and retrain the model before deployment
- **Option 4:** Configure the system to automatically route any triage output with confidence below a defined threshold to mandatory human escalation

Which sequence CORRECTLY ranks these options from MOST to LEAST effective according to established risk mitigation hierarchy principles?

A) Option 2 → Option 4 → Option 1 → Option 3 — human oversight always supersedes technical controls, which supersede source-level corrections and informational measures

B) Option 1 → Option 4 → Option 2 → Option 3 — stakeholder notification must precede any technical or procedural controls to ensure informed consent within the system

C) Option 4 → Option 2 → Option 3 → Option 1 — automated safeguards are more reliable than human judgment, which is more reliable than retraining, which is more reliable than informational controls

D) Option 3 → Option 4 → Option 2 → Option 1 — eliminating the risk at source is most effective; engineering controls that reduce exposure come next; then procedural human review; informational measures are least effective and used only as a last resort

**Q8.** [Domain IV.A | Bloom: Apply]

A financial services firm wants to deploy a large language model to assist compliance officers in retrieving and summarizing the firm's internal regulatory policies, guidance documents, and enforcement history. The business requirement specifies that every output must be traceable to a specific source document, and that the retrieval corpus must remain current as policies are updated — without requiring model retraining every time a document changes.

Which deployment architecture BEST meets these requirements?

A) Fine-tuning the base LLM on the firm's full internal policy corpus, because fine-tuning encodes firm-specific knowledge into model weights, ensuring outputs always reflect firm documents without needing a separate retrieval layer

B) Deploying the base LLM via API without modification, because general-purpose language models already contain sufficient legal and regulatory knowledge to assist compliance officers without further customization

C) Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), because this architecture grounds the model's outputs in documents retrieved in real-time from the firm's knowledge base — enabling citation of specific source documents, supporting output traceability, and allowing the corpus to be updated without retraining the underlying model

D) Building a fully proprietary model trained exclusively on public regulatory data, because restricting training to publicly available data eliminates the legal and confidentiality risks associated with processing internal documents during fine-tuning

**Q9.** [Domain IV.C | Bloom: Analyze]

A risk manager at a telecommunications company monitors an AI-powered customer churn prediction model trained on pre-pandemic behavioral data. Over the past six months, the model's prediction accuracy for customers on remote work plans — a segment that grew from 9% to 37% of the customer base following permanent hybrid work adoption — has declined from 82% to 55%, while aggregate model accuracy across the full customer base appears stable at 79%.

Which conclusion MOST accurately characterizes what this pattern represents?

A) A model security incident: the accuracy decline for the remote work segment indicates that adversarial actors are deliberately manipulating input data for this group to prevent accurate churn predictions and avoid targeted retention offers

B) Concept drift affecting a specific sub-population: the behavioral patterns of the remote work segment have shifted sufficiently from the training distribution that the model's learned associations no longer generalize — requiring targeted retraining on current data from this segment

C) A training data quality issue: the original training dataset was biased against remote work customers, and the growing size of the segment is making this pre-existing training deficiency increasingly visible in aggregate metrics

D) Normal model performance variation within acceptable tolerance: a decline from 82% to 55% on a segment now representing 37% of the customer base is within expected statistical variance for production AI systems and does not warrant remediation
