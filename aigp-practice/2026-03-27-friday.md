**Q1.** [Domain IV.A | Bloom: Apply] 🎯 Case Study

A healthcare startup is selecting an AI model to assist radiologists in detecting early-stage lung nodules on CT scans. The regulatory team classifies the system as high-risk under the EU AI Act. The CTO presents three model options:

- **Option 1:** A large proprietary multimodal model from a major cloud provider, accessed via API, with state-of-the-art accuracy benchmarks on public datasets but limited information about training data composition
- **Option 2:** A smaller, specialized open-source model trained specifically on pulmonary imaging datasets, deployable on-premise, with published model cards and documented performance across demographic subgroups
- **Option 3:** A general-purpose large language model fine-tuned with medical literature, deployed via RAG architecture pulling from the hospital's own diagnostic database

From a governance perspective, which factor should be the PRIMARY consideration when making this deployment decision?

A) The overall accuracy benchmark on public datasets, since regulatory approval primarily depends on demonstrating the highest possible detection rate regardless of other governance factors

B) The total cost of ownership, as the organization's fiduciary duty to shareholders outweighs governance considerations when selecting between equivalent AI solutions

C) The ability to fulfill high-risk system obligations — including transparency into training data, performance documentation across demographic subgroups, human oversight integration, and technical documentation requirements — which directly determines whether the organization can achieve and demonstrate regulatory compliance

D) The deployment speed, since the EU AI Act's Annex III deadline of August 2026 requires all high-risk systems to be fully deployed before the enforcement date to qualify for grandfathering provisions

**Q4.** [Domain IV.B | Bloom: Apply] 🎯 Case Study

A European logistics company is evaluating an AI-powered route optimization system from a third-party vendor. During vendor due diligence, the governance team reviews the licensing agreement and discovers the following terms:

- The vendor retains the right to update the model remotely without prior notice to the deployer
- The agreement does not include audit rights or access to bias testing results
- The contract specifies that the vendor's total liability is capped at the annual license fee
- Performance SLAs are based on uptime only, with no accuracy or fairness metrics

From a vendor risk management perspective, which contract term poses the GREATEST governance risk?

A) The liability cap at the annual license fee, because this limits the deployer's ability to recover financial damages in the event of a system failure

B) The right to update the model remotely without notice, because uncontrolled model updates could alter the system's behavior, risk profile, and compliance status without the deployer's knowledge or ability to conduct pre-deployment assessment of the changed system

C) The uptime-only SLA, because availability metrics are the most important performance indicator for logistics systems and should include a 99.99% guarantee

D) The lack of bias testing results, because bias is only a concern for AI systems that process personal data of natural persons and a logistics route optimizer does not involve personal data
