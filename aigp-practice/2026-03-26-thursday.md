**Q1.** [Domain IV.B | Bloom: Apply] 🎯 Case Study

NovaTech, a mid-sized insurance company, is evaluating three vendors to deploy a claims fraud detection AI system. During due diligence, the procurement team reviews each vendor's documentation:

- **Vendor A** offers a proprietary cloud-hosted model with strong accuracy metrics but refuses to share information about training data sources, model architecture, or bias testing results, citing trade secrets
- **Vendor B** provides an open-source model with full transparency into training data and architecture, but has no commercial support agreement or defined SLA for uptime
- **Vendor C** offers a proprietary on-premise model with detailed model cards, independent audit reports, and contractual commitments to bias testing — but at 3x the cost of Vendor A

From an AI governance and vendor risk management perspective, what is the PRIMARY concern with selecting Vendor A?

A) The higher cost of alternative vendors may exceed the organization's AI budget, making Vendor A the only financially viable option regardless of governance considerations

B) The lack of transparency into training data, architecture, and bias testing creates an unacceptable governance gap — the deploying organization cannot fulfill its own obligations for risk management, bias monitoring, and regulatory compliance when it has no visibility into the system's development practices

C) Proprietary cloud-hosted models always have higher latency than on-premise solutions, which will negatively impact the real-time fraud detection performance required by the insurance industry

D) Open-source alternatives like Vendor B are always preferable from a governance perspective because they provide complete transparency, which automatically satisfies all regulatory requirements

**Q5.** [Domain IV.B | Bloom: Analyze] 🎯 Case Study

- The system may disproportionately reject applications from self-employed individuals and gig workers due to training data bias toward traditional employment patterns
- Rejected applicants receive only a numerical risk score without explanation of contributing factors
- There is no mechanism for applicants to contest an automated rejection or request human review

Which fundamental right is MOST directly threatened by the lack of a contestation and human review mechanism?

A) The right to privacy — because the automated processing of financial data without a review mechanism constitutes unlawful surveillance of individuals' economic activities

B) The right to an effective remedy and a fair trial — because individuals affected by AI-driven decisions must have access to meaningful human review and the ability to contest decisions that significantly affect them

C) The right to freedom of expression — because the inability to contest automated decisions restricts applicants' ability to communicate their financial circumstances to the decision-maker

D) The right to property — because the denial of a loan directly constitutes a deprivation of property under fundamental rights law
