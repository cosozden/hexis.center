A) Immediately discard the entire training dataset and restart development using exclusively synthetic data to eliminate any risk of proxy discrimination in the revised model

B) Deploy the model as planned but mandate human review for all cases where the model's score influences a hiring decision — this satisfies the human oversight requirement while preserving development timelines

**Q5.** [Domain II.C | Bloom: Apply]

A retailer deploys a customer service kiosk in a European shopping mall with a real-time camera system that analyzes shoppers' facial expressions and classifies their apparent emotional state (frustrated, content, neutral) to adjust product recommendations accordingly. The retailer argues no consent is required because the system does not perform biometric identification — it only recognizes emotional states, not individual identities.

Under the EU AI Act, which statement MOST accurately describes the retailer's compliance obligations?

A) The retailer's position is correct — Article 50 transparency obligations are triggered only by biometric identification systems; emotion recognition systems that do not link emotional data to individual identities fall outside the scope of Article 50 disclosure requirements

B) The system is prohibited under Article 5 because any deployment of real-time camera feeds in publicly accessible spaces for behavioral analysis constitutes a prohibited AI practice regardless of the system's specific function

C) The retailer must inform individuals that they are subject to an emotion recognition system — Article 50(3) specifically requires transparency disclosure for emotion recognition systems regardless of whether the system identifies individuals

D) The system requires conformity assessment and registration in the EU AI Act database before deployment because any AI system that interacts with consumers in physical public spaces is automatically classified as high-risk under Annex III

**Q6.** [Domain IV.A | Bloom: Apply] — Case Study

A hospital informatics team is evaluating two architectures for a clinical decision support system:

**Architecture P (Fine-tuning):** Incorporates 50,000 curated clinical case notes and the hospital's treatment protocols into an existing foundation model through additional training, updating the model's weights.

**Architecture Q (RAG):** Retrieves relevant passages from the hospital's regularly updated clinical knowledge base at inference time without modifying the underlying model's weights.

The governance team's priorities are: (1) keeping clinical knowledge current without scheduled retraining cycles, and (2) being able to trace which specific source documents informed each recommendation.

Which architecture BEST meets these governance priorities?

A) Architecture Q (RAG), because clinical knowledge stays current by updating the knowledge base rather than retraining the model, and retrieved source documents can be surfaced alongside recommendations to directly support traceability

B) Architecture P (fine-tuning), because incorporating clinical case notes directly into model weights ensures the model has deeply internalized clinical reasoning patterns, producing more consistent and auditable recommendations than dynamic retrieval

C) Architecture P (fine-tuning), because fine-tuned models do not require an external knowledge base infrastructure, reducing the number of components that can fail and simplifying security governance

D) Architecture Q (RAG), because RAG systems always have lower computational cost at inference than fine-tuned models, making them preferable for hospital environments with constrained IT budgets
